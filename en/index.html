FAQ

Questions parents ask

Do you teach the Suzuki method?

Yes. Most young beginners start on a Suzuki-based path from Book 1, with note reading and ear training added alongside, so students can also read music and play in a school orchestra.

If your child is already working through a Suzuki book, lessons continue from exactly where they are — the book is checked in the trial lesson.

Can you prepare my child for school orchestra or All-State auditions?

Yes. Audition excerpts, scales, sight-reading and the tone and rhythm a school program expects are a regular part of lessons, and All-State audition coaching is available.

Students have been placed in middle school orchestra sections after focused audition preparation.

How do online lessons work, and can you really see bowing on a screen?

Lessons run on Zoom. A phone or laptop on a stand, roughly chest height, with enough room to draw the bow, is enough. The camera angle is checked at the start of the first lesson so bowing and left-hand position are visible, and headphones help the student hear tone clearly.

A stable connection matters far more than an expensive camera or microphone.

How do I pay, and how do I book a lesson?

International students pay by PayPal or Wise. Families in Korea can pay by bank transfer. Lesson rates are shared upon inquiry based on level and format.

Start with a 30-minute trial lesson, then choose a weekly slot. US after-school hours (EST 6:00–10:00 PM, PST 3:00–7:00 PM) are kept open for students in the United States.
